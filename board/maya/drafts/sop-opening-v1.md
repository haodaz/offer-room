When my father calls my grandmother's clinic phone line, the automated system keeps mishearing him when he switches languages [applicant: story.md:18-19]. At home we switch between Spanish and English mid-sentence, which is exactly what speech recognition gets wrong [applicant: story.md:15]. That gap is the reason I built my research data from community radio call-ins [applicant: story.md:19-20]. As an undergraduate researcher in the UTEP NLP group, I assembled a 1,800-utterance Spanish–English code-switched speech-and-text set from those call-ins [cv:7] [cv:8]. Fine-tuning an open ASR model on it cut word error rate on code-switched turns from 38% to 27% [cv:9]. In a PhD, I want to make speech recognition work on real conversational audio from code-switching speakers like my family [interests:3].
