# Priya Raman: who she is (fictional)

Every agent reads this before working with Priya, so she is the same person in every room. Facts about her record
are in cv.md and the other files; this is the person behind them. Never contradict it, and never add to it in a
reply: if something is not here, ask her.

## In one line
A careful, first-in-the-family-to-go-abroad NLP student from Tiruchirappalli who wants language technology to work
for people like her grandmother, and who can afford exactly two applications.

## Where she comes from
- Grew up in Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu. Father is a clerk at a cooperative bank; mother teaches maths at a
  government school. Younger sister in class 11.
- First in her family to apply abroad. Her parents support it, and are anxious about the distance and the cost.
- Speaks Tamil at home, English at college, conversational Hindi from two years of hostel friends.

## Why a PhD
- Her grandmother speaks only Tamil and mixes in English words for medicines and phone menus. Every chatbot and voice
  assistant the family tried failed her. That is the moment Priya points to when people ask why NLP.
- Her code-switch evaluation set (cv.md) started as an attempt to show, with numbers, how badly open models fail on
  the way her family actually talks.
- She likes evaluation because it makes failure visible. She is less sure about speech, which she tried once in a
  course project and found hard.

## What she has to work with
- Budget: the family has set aside 30,000 rupees for applications, about 350 US dollars. After TOEFL score reports,
  that covers two application fees. A fee waiver would mean a third school.
- Time: final-year B.Tech, TA for 120 students this semester, reading group on Thursdays. Evenings and Sundays are
  for applications.
- Letters: Venkatesan and Mehra are solid. Krishnan only knows her course project and has not replied.

## What worries her
- Picking two schools and getting both wrong.
- Writing about herself: her SOP draft is modest to the point of hiding her best work.
- Visa timing and whether a stipend really covers rent in an expensive US city.
- Being the only person in a lab from her background.

## Five years after an admit
- Wants to be doing evaluation and adaptation work for Indian languages, ideally with a lab that sends people both
  to industry research and to faculty jobs.
- Open to coming back to India to build an Indic-language team at a research lab, or to teach.
- Would choose a kind advisor with a smaller name over a famous one who is never around.

## How she talks
- Polite, precise, a little formal in English. Asks for exact dates and exact numbers.
- Says "I am not sure this is strong enough" about things that are strong. Needs to hear concretely why.
- Writes in English in the room, sometimes a Tamil word for family.

## How to work with her
- Lead with the number or the date she asked for, then the reason.
- When her work is strong, say so plainly and show the line that proves it.
- Never make a decision for her; she decides, and she will tell her parents why.
