# Maya Torres: who she is (fictional)

Every agent reads this before working with Maya, so she is the same person in every room. Facts about her record
are in cv.md and the other files; this is the person behind them. Never contradict it, and never add to it in a
reply: if something is not here, ask her.

## In one line
A first-generation CS student from El Paso who fixes speech recognition for people who talk the way her family does,
and who will not apply anywhere she cannot afford to apply or afford to live.

## Where she comes from
- Grew up in El Paso, Texas, on the border. Mother is a home health aide; father drives for a regional freight
  company. Younger brother is a high-school junior.
- First in her family to go to college. Federal Pell Grant every year; lives at home to save rent.
- Spanish and English are both native for her; at home they switch mid-sentence, which is exactly what ASR gets wrong.

## Why a PhD
- Her father calls a clinic phone line for her grandmother, and the automated system keeps mishearing him when he
  switches languages. That is the problem she wants to fix, and the reason her research set comes from community
  radio call-ins (cv.md).
- Her REU summer showed her a second problem she cares about: LLMs give less reliable health answers in Spanish.
- She did not know a PhD was paid until her advisor, Prof. Ortega, told her in her second year.

## What she has to work with
- Money: about 300 dollars saved for applications, earned from tutoring. Fee waivers are not a nice-to-have; they
  decide how many schools she applies to.
- Time: senior year, about 10 hours a week of peer tutoring she cannot drop.
- Letters: Ortega and Brandt know her research well. Kim needs a short summary of her project before writing.

## What worries her
- Fees, and whether waivers are real or a form that goes nowhere.
- Moving far from her family; her brother is applying to college next year.
- Sounding like she does not belong next to applicants from big-name schools.

## Five years after an admit
- Wants to lead speech work for bilingual communities, either at a research lab or as a professor at a
  Hispanic-Serving Institution like the one she came from.
- Cares a lot about where a lab's students end up, and whether they finish.

## How she talks
- Direct, quick, a bit funny. Asks "how much does it cost" and "what's the catch".
- Writes in English, sometimes switches to Spanish mid-message. Answer in the language she used for that message.

## How to work with her
- Money first, then fit: say what something costs and what can make it free, with the eligibility line.
- Treat her code-switching data as the asset it is; do not let her apologise for her school.
- Never make a decision for her; she decides.
